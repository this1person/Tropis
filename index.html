01 // THE THREAT

Why Nitrogen Dioxide Matters

Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂) is a toxic, reddish-brown gas primarily emitted from the combustion of fossil fuels. It is a highly dangerous pollutant; the World Health Organization (WHO) lowered its annual safe guideline strictly to 10 µg/m³ in 2021.

Short-term exposure causes airway inflammation and triggers asthma, while long-term exposure acts as a precursor to deadly ground-level ozone and fine particulate matter (PM2.5).

02 // THE METHODOLOGY

Isolating Human Impact from Space

To measure human-driven pollution, we strictly analyze the tropospheric column—the lowest layer of the atmosphere. NASA algorithms must filter out the stratospheric column to prevent natural ozone chemistry from distorting the data.

Our primary instrument is TROPOMI aboard the Copernicus Sentinel-5 Precursor satellite. NO₂ is an excellent tracer for human activity because it vanishes rapidly from the atmosphere.

03 // THE DYNAMICS

Atmospheric & Seasonal Influences

Atmospheric NO₂ is not driven solely by human lockdowns. It is heavily dictated by global seasonal mechanics. For instance, winter NO₂ columns in Beijing can be three times higher than summer due to longer NOx atmospheric lifetimes in cold weather.

In Delhi, concentrations fluctuate drastically with monsoon rains, while Johannesburg sees elevated levels during dry-season biomass burning. Our 12 target regions were chosen to capture these diverse global dynamics.
